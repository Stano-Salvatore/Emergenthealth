Emergenthealth

A read of the app as it stands today — 26 routes, six themes, eight accents — and a proposal for the one change that fixes most of what feels off: giving colour a job. Nothing in the codebase has been touched. This is the plan, for your sign-off.

What I read

The token sheet (globals.css), the app shell, sidebar, bottom nav, base card and button, the mobile Today view, Quick log, and a full scan of every hard-coded colour class across the 26 dashboard routes — 381 hits.

Your brief: it should stay colourful, but a thing should keep its colour. Agreed — and that turns out to be the root of the inconsistency, not a symptom of it.

What I assumed

  • Phone first, Play Store audience; web is the same product at a wider viewport.
  • Emergy stays, and stays the centre button.
  • Your daily jobs are: sleep arrives on its own, you log intake, you glance.
  • IA may be restructured; the dark theme and the widget grid are fair game but I am not proposing to remove either.

Finding 01 — the big one

Colour is decoration, not language

Every hue in the app is doing three or four unrelated jobs at once, and each metric is encoded differently depending on which screen you are standing on. Nothing here is a bug — each choice is locally sensible. Read together, they cancel out.

Worst single case · sleep

One metric, three encodings, two taps apart. On mobile Today the week's sleep bars are indigo. On Health, hours of sleep colour green / amber / red against a 7h goal, while the sleep score on the same card colours green / amber / red against 85 / 70. So a good night can read indigo, green and amber within one session — and nothing on screen says which scale is in play.

Second worst · heart

Resting HR is a red heart icon on Health and on the Overview metric box — but its sparkline on mobile Today is drawn indigo (#818cf8), the same hue as the sleep bars, while HRV takes pink. Meanwhile red is also error, overdue, expense, Sunday, delete, and high blood pressure. Red is the most overloaded token in the app.

The proposal

Two palettes that never touch

Split colour into identity (which domain am I looking at — fixed forever, theme-independent) and status (is this good — only ever three steps, and never a domain hue). Then one rule: a screen may tint a number by status, or by identity, never by both.

Identity — locked per domain

Sleep is this indigo on every screen, in every theme, forever. Charts, icons, dots, bars, timeline.

Status — three steps, nothing else

Applied to the goal ring, a chip, or a rule under a number — never to the metric's own glyph, so it can't be mistaken for identity.

Consequences

  • Money stops being green/red: amount stays neutral, direction is carried by the sign and the arrow. Income green + expense red currently collides with habit-done green and error red.
  • Intake's twelve drink hues collapse to one family — water is the domain hue, everything else is a tint or an outline of it, with the label doing the identifying.
  • Delete, error and overdue keep red, so red must stop being a metric colour: heart becomes rose, not red.
  • The nine .card-* gradients in globals.css get derived from the domain hue, so they survive a theme switch instead of staying emerald in Forest and rose in Ocean.

Information architecture

Twenty-six rooms, one hallway

The drawer lists 26 destinations, eleven of them hidden by default; the bottom bar pins five, and only two of those five overlap with what a new user sees in the drawer. Nothing tells you that Medications lives inside Intake or that Body lives inside Health — that knowledge is in a code comment. For a Play Store audience I would restructure to four rooms plus settings, with integrations demoted from destinations to sources.

Today — as it is

  • Drawer: Overview, Brief, Emergy, Check-in, Habits, Garden, Intake & Meds, Health & Body, Calendar, Reminders, Finances, Focus, Journal, Location, Settings — then eleven more behind Customize (This Week, Timeline, Trends, Streaks, Gmail, Reading, Strava, Last.fm, RescueTime, Subscriptions, Home).
  • Bottom bar: Home, Finances or Check-in, Emergy, Habits, Settings.
  • Six pages are one integration each; three are read-only report pages.
